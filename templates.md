# BD Templates — Web3/Crypto

Generic templates for every stage of the BD lifecycle. Fill in `[Your Name]`, `[Your Org]`, and product names before sending — and adapt tone to the specific person. These are starting points, not scripts. Crypto-native audiences can smell a canned message instantly.

---

## Outreach Message Templates

### Template 1 — Protocol / project team with an acute current need
**Context:** Cold-ish outreach. Best sent via X DM or team Discord if you've already been present in their community.

> Hey [name] — been following [specific recent thing they shipped or said].
>
> I'm [your name], working on growth and BD at [your org]. We run [product 1] and [product 2].
>
> We're building out [relevant content/program] ahead of [relevant event/upgrade/launch] — specifically for [underserved segment/market] where there's almost no good information about [relevant topic]. Thought there might be an interesting angle for [their project] here. Would love to hear what distribution challenges look like from your side.
>
> No pitch — genuinely curious whether our audiences overlap in a useful way.

---

### Template 2 — Staking / infra protocol
**Context:** They have existing communications teams. Reach the right person — usually DevRel, ecosystem, or growth. Not the CEO.

> Hi [name] — [your name] from [your org] here. You might know us from [product]. We're focused on [mission/positioning].
>
> With [relevant upgrade/event] approaching, we're producing [content type] for [target market/region] — a market where there's very little clear information in local languages. Given [their project]'s footprint there, thought this might be worth a conversation.
>
> Happy to share what we're building. Would a quick async exchange work for you?

---

### Template 3 — L1/L2 protocol
**Context:** Protocol teams are busy and receive a lot of BD outreach. Be specific about what you've noticed in their work.

> Hey [name] — saw [specific thing: recent regional hackathon they ran / dev grant they announced / post about builder growth]. Makes sense given [their chain]'s positioning.
>
> I'm at [your org] — we run [product], reaching builders in [N] countries who are actively looking for their next [grant/bounty/role/opportunity].
>
> A few teams we work with have used [product] to reach builders in markets they weren't penetrating through their own channels. Wondering if that's a problem [their chain] is solving for right now.

---

### Template 4 — Commercial infra/tooling company
**Context:** These are commercial companies, not protocol teams. A slightly more direct BD approach is fine.

> Hi [name] — [your name] from [your org]. We run [product 1] and [product 2].
>
> We're producing [content type] ahead of [relevant event] — aimed at [target audience]. High intent, forced-action moment.
>
> Wanted to explore whether a sponsorship slot in that content makes sense for [their company]. Happy to send over audience details if useful.

---

### Template 5 — Institutional / TradFi (longer, more formal)
**Context:** These contacts expect more formal communication. Email is better than DM.

Subject: [Your org] — ecosystem partnership inquiry

> Dear [name],
>
> I'm reaching out from [your org], [one-line positioning/credibility anchor]. We build [product 1] and [product 2], reaching [audience description].
>
> With [their institution] expanding into [relevant crypto/on-chain activity], I wanted to explore whether our community position might be useful to your team. We offer something that's hard to access directly — genuine credibility and trust within the builder community, not just market access.
>
> Specifically, I'd be curious to understand what your team's priorities are in the ecosystem right now and whether there's a fit around research collaboration, talent pipeline access, or editorial presence in our coverage.
>
> Would a brief conversation make sense?

## Follow-Up Templates

### After no response (1–2 weeks later)
Keep it short. One line.

> Hey [name] — bumping this up in case it got buried. Still happy to connect if the timing is better now.

### After a positive first conversation
> Great talking through this. Quick summary of what I took away: [2–3 bullet points of what they said they care about]. From our side, [specific product] seems like the most relevant starting point. I'll put together a short proposal and send it over — should have it to you by [date]. Let me know if anything I outlined didn't land right.

---

## Proposal Format

Keep proposals to one page or one shared doc. Never a deck in the first instance.

---

**Proposal: [Partnership type] — [Org name] x [Your Org]**

**The problem we're solving for [Org name]**
[1–2 sentences. Their language, not ours. What specific challenge does this address?]

**What [your org] offers**
[Product + specific deliverables + timeline. Be concrete: "One sponsored slot in [publication] Issue #X, distributed to [audience descriptor], running [date range]." Not vague: "newsletter partnership."]

**What we need from you**
[Clear ask: budget range, content assets, co-promotion commitments. Don't leave this implicit.]

**What success looks like**
[Specific metric or outcome. "At least [N] applications to your listing." "Coverage in front of [region] operators ahead of [event]." Not: "increased visibility."]

**Next step**
[One clear action with a name and a date. "I'll send a draft agreement by [date]." "Can you confirm budget range by end of week so we can scope this properly?"]

---

## Renewal Conversation Opener

Send 4–6 weeks before the deal end date, after delivering a brief outcome report.

> [Name] — quick update on how the [campaign/listing/sponsorship] performed: [2–3 honest numbers or outcomes].
>
> Based on that, [what worked well / what you'd adjust next time].
>
> We have [next cycle/edition/cohort] coming up in [timeframe] — wanted to get your thinking on whether a continuation makes sense before we open that slot to others.
